# Project Episteme initial design

Project Episteme will formalize the second edition of CWM in Lean 4 with mathlib as a step toward Bourbaki 2.0. A record for each source item will point to the Lean proof and its hypotheses. The Joyal–Street and Tôhoku tracks extend the same method to free braided categories and derived functors.

## Scope

| Source | Track and role |
| --- | --- |
| CWM I–V, IX–X | **1. Category (Mac Lane 2.0)**. Work from basic categories through Yoneda, adjunctions, and limits to Kan extensions. |
| CWM VII, XI | **2. Monoidal category (Mac Lane 2.0)**. Cover monoidal categories, coherence, monoid objects, and applications. Use the Joyal–Street construction for braided and symmetric coherence. |
| CWM VIII and the Tôhoku paper | **3. Abelian category (Grothendieck’s Tôhoku 2.0)**. Build on CWM’s abelian categories and diagram lemmas to cover the Tôhoku exactness axioms, injectives, and derived functors. |
| CWM VI, XII | Later Category work. Chapter VI covers monads and Beck’s theorem. Chapter XII covers internal categories, 2-categories, and bicategories. Bring forward individual prerequisites when another track needs them. |
| alg-d.com, Chapters 0–2 | Primary Category source while the CWM text is not available at item level (see the [Category strategy](Project_Episteme_Category_Strategy.md)). Share proofs for results also found in CWM. Give the applications their own source IDs and completion criteria. |

Chapter references follow the second edition. The CWM reference for braided coherence at the end of the Joyal–Street roadmap is **XI §5**, p. 263. [CWM table of contents](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/bfm%3A978-1-4757-4721-8/1) [Joyal–Street roadmap](Project_Episteme_Joyal_Street_Coherence_Roadmap.md)

**Verify the main text of CWM first.** Each chapter’s first verification pass covers definitions, theorems, and representative examples. Record each exercise’s source and statement during that pass, then prove the exercises in a later phase. This scope applies to CWM across all three tracks. The Category track is the exception: alg-d.com Chapters 0–2 take CWM’s place until CWM can be cataloged item by item. CWM items are then mapped to the same Lean declarations, and their hypotheses are reviewed separately.

## Mapping sources to Lean

Record each source’s edition, chapter, section, and page. Catalog definitions, theorems, corollaries, mathematical examples, and exercises. Check domains, quantifiers, morphism directions, naturality, and smallness assumptions. Point directly to matching mathlib declarations and prove bridging lemmas when formulations differ. For CWM III §2, compare the natural correspondence between morphisms from a representable presheaf to a presheaf and the value of that presheaf at the representing object with `CategoryTheory.yonedaEquiv` and its naturality results. [CWM table of contents](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/bfm%3A978-1-4757-4721-8/1) [mathlib’s Yoneda lemma](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/CategoryTheory/Yoneda.html)

| Field | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| Source and ID | Use an ID such as `CWM2-III-02-YONEDA`. Keep distinct IDs for CWM, `ALGD`, `JS`, and `TOHOKU` items. |
| Statement and dependencies | A paraphrase of the source statement, IDs of prerequisites, and differences between mathlib’s assumptions and the source’s. |
| Lean | The mathlib commit, required imports, fully qualified names, and the declarations for any bridging results. |
| Status | `unreviewed`, `existing`, `bridged`, `new-proved`, `blocked`, or `non-propositional`. |
| Verification | Source review, type and build checks in the pinned environment, and an axiom audit of key declarations. |

Track the number of cataloged items and the number verified. Verification requires checking the declaration’s type and proof in the pinned environment and reviewing the match against the source. When several source items point to one declaration, review the hypotheses for each item. [Initial Plan and ledger format](Project_Episteme_Initial_Plan.md)

## 1. Category (Mac Lane 2.0)

Develop the language and universal properties of CWM I–V. Use the special limits of Chapter IX to reach Kan extensions in Chapter X. The Yoneda lemma in CWM III §2 is the proposed first Lean example. Record the source, type, hypotheses, and verification results for one proposition so later tracks have a worked ledger entry. The [Category strategy](Project_Episteme_Category_Strategy.md) sets the working order (alg-d first while the CWM text is unavailable), the conventions, and the ledger rules.

| Phase | Design | Acceptance criteria |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1A. Foundations | Cover CWM I–II. Use `CategoryTheory.Category`, `Functor`, `NatTrans`, opposite categories, functor categories, and comma categories. Make size assumptions explicit when treating the category of small categories. | Check identity and composition, naturality, duality through opposite categories, and a concrete comma category example in the pinned environment. |
| 1B. Universal properties | Cover CWM III–V. Treat representability, Yoneda, limits and colimits, adjunctions, and adjoint functor theorems. Relate universal arrows and natural hom-set equivalences to units and counits. | Check Yoneda naturality, the triangle identities, and the assumptions for existence, preservation, and creation of limits. |
| 1C. Special limits and Kan extensions | Cover CWM IX–X. Use filtered diagrams, final functors, ends, and coends to develop the left and right universal properties, pointwise constructions, the coend formula, and density. | Check the direction of the comparison maps, naturality of the hom-set equivalences, uniqueness up to canonical isomorphism, diagram sizes for pointwise formulas, and the existence conditions for the two adjoints to precomposition. |
| 1D. Applications and remaining chapters | Catalog simplicial sets, adjoint functor theorems, sheafification, the “logarithm” of a category, filtered categories, ends and coends, co-Yoneda, profunctors, and Isbell duality from the alg-d.com notes. Bring in CWM VI and XII as prerequisites allow. | Report CWM coverage and supplementary coverage. Use the PDF-by-PDF inventory and review in the Initial Plan to establish full coverage of the alg-d.com notes, Chapters 0–2. |

Given `K : C ⥤ D` and `F : C ⥤ E`, the left Kan extension has comparison map `F ⟶ K ⋙ Lan`, and the right Kan extension has comparison map `K ⋙ Ran ⟶ F`. At `d : D`, compute the left extension by a colimit over `CostructuredArrow K d` and the right extension by a limit over `StructuredArrow d K`, when those colimits and limits exist. Check the general universal properties and pointwise constructions separately. The adjunctions `Lan ⊣ K* ⊣ Ran` require the respective extensions for every `F`. [KanExtension.Basic](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/CategoryTheory/Functor/KanExtension/Basic.html) [KanExtension.Pointwise](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/CategoryTheory/Functor/KanExtension/Pointwise.html) [KanExtension.Adjunction](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/CategoryTheory/Functor/KanExtension/Adjunction.html)

## 2. Monoidal category (Mac Lane 2.0)

Match the associators, unitors, and monoid objects of CWM VII to mathlib. Prove braided coherence for CWM XI through the Joyal–Street free construction. The syntax of structural morphisms uses parenthesized tensor expressions, and the braid model uses words of colors as objects. Interpret the syntax in a general monoidal category by fixing a bracketing and using associators and unitors to change brackets. [Joyal–Street roadmap](Project_Episteme_Joyal_Street_Coherence_Roadmap.md) [Joyal–Street 1991, §3](https://core.ac.uk/download/pdf/82659437.pdf)

| Phase | Design | Acceptance criteria |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2A. Monoidal categories | Reuse `MonoidalCategory`, `FreeMonoidalCategory`, `BraidedCategory`, and `SymmetricCategory`. Check the structure isomorphisms of strong monoidal functors and the condition for preserving a braiding. | Check the pentagon, triangle, and hexagon identities and rebracket expressions using associators and unitors. Catalog the monoid objects, actions, closed structures, and other applications in CWM VII. |
| 2B. Artin braid groups | Use the presentation of `B_n`, adjacent generators, the homomorphism `B_n → Equiv.Perm (Fin n)`, juxtaposition, and block crossings. Review version, license, and mathlib compatibility before selecting an external Lean implementation. | Check the Artin relations, functoriality of juxtaposition, triviality of `B_0` and `B_1`, and the group isomorphism between `B_2` and `Multiplicative ℤ`. |
| 2C. Free colored braid category | Take `List A` as the object type. A morphism is a braid between words of equal length whose permutation carries the source word to the target word. Tensor uses concatenation and juxtaposition. | Prove the laws for color transport, composition order, the empty word, naturality, and both hexagons. Record equality transports for concatenation and the comparison isomorphisms for interpretation in general monoidal categories. |
| 2D. Freeness and coherence | Restrict strong braided monoidal functors to words of length one and prove an equivalence with `Discrete A ⥤ C`. Include the correspondence on natural transformations. | Prove freeness for one generator and for arbitrary colors. Prove that parallel structural morphisms are equal in every interpretation exactly when their braids agree. |
| 2E. Symmetric case and extensions | Identify the quotient imposing `σᵢ² = 1` with `Equiv.Perm (Fin n)` and use the colored permutation category to obtain symmetric coherence. Later candidates include freeness on a generating category, comparison with geometric isotopy, and tactics. | Show that the double crossing differs from the identity in `B₂` and becomes the identity in the symmetric quotient. Prove that parallel structural morphisms are universally equal in symmetric monoidal categories exactly when their permutations agree. |

Braided coherence compares interpretations in **all braided monoidal categories**. Equal braids give equal morphisms in each interpretation. For the converse, interpret the morphisms in the free colored braid category itself. For a generating category, label each strand with a morphism of that category and prove the corresponding freeness result. [mathlib monoidal coherence](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/CategoryTheory/Monoidal/Free/Coherence.html) [mathlib braidings and functors](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/CategoryTheory/Monoidal/Braided/Basic.html) [Joyal–Street 1991, Theorems 3.1–3.2](https://core.ac.uk/download/pdf/82659437.pdf)

## 3. Abelian category (Grothendieck’s Tôhoku 2.0)

Use mathlib’s `Preadditive`, `Abelian`, and `ShortComplex` for the material shared by CWM VIII and Tôhoku §1. Add the AB conditions and a generator, pass through enough injectives, and reach the right-derived functors of §2. When mapping a result from the paper to mathlib, record whether the proof also follows the original argument. [Grothendieck’s 1957 paper](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/tmj1949/9/2/9_2_119/_article/-char/ja/) [Barr’s English translation](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/files/BarrTranslOf-GrothedieckTohoku.pdf)

| Phase | Design | Acceptance criteria |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3A. Abelian categories and exactness | Compare zero objects, finite biproducts, kernels and cokernels, and images and coimages. Use `ShortComplex.Exact` and `ShortComplex.ShortExact`. | Check the correspondence between image equaling kernel and vanishing homology. In `ModuleCat R`, verify the split short exact sequence `M → M ⊞ N → N` and an application of the five lemma or snake lemma. |
| 3B. Grothendieck categories and injective resolutions | Compare the AB conditions, generators, and enough injectives from Tôhoku §§1.5, 1.9, and 1.10. State the separator, `LocallySmall.{w}`, and the existence and exactness of filtered colimits of size `w` in `IsGrothendieckAbelian.{w}`. | Map Theorem 1.10.1 to mathlib’s `EnoughInjectives` result. In `ModuleCat R`, check that every object admits a monomorphism into an injective object and has an injective resolution. |
| 3C. Right-derived functors | Use injective resolutions to obtain `F.rightDerived n` for an additive functor `F : C ⥤ D` between abelian categories when `C` has enough injectives. Use finite limit preservation for the comparison `R⁰F ≅ F`. | For `F = Hom_R(M, -)`, check the natural isomorphism `R⁰F ≅ F` and vanishing `RⁿF(I) ≅ 0` at an injective object `I` for `n > 0`. |
| 3D. δ-functors and applications | Investigate connecting morphisms, long exact sequences, and universal δ-functors from Tôhoku §§2.1–2.3. Initial applications include `Ext` and sheaf cohomology on small sites. Share the sheafification and size assumptions with the Category ledger. | In the selected application, check naturality and exactness of the connecting morphisms. Inspect the pinned revision to determine what can be reused for the general theorem about additive left exact functors. |

## Implementation order and acceptance criteria

1. **Use the pinned environment.** Project Episteme uses the Lean toolchain and dependency revisions from Project Ainos. Use `lake build` for a reproducible build. Check proposed declarations in the pinned source and confirm their minimal imports. [Lean toolchain](../lean-toolchain) [Lake manifest](../lake-manifest.json)
2. **Verify one CWM theorem.** Start with the Yoneda lemma as the proposed first result. Put the source paraphrase, declaration and type, size assumptions, build result, and axiom audit in one ledger record. The Category track did this with alg-d’s 米田の補題 (`ALGD-C1-YONEDA`), the counterpart of CWM III §2. The CWM record follows once CWM is cataloged.
3. **Build the common foundations.** Start with the basic language and universal properties of the Category track. The Monoidal category track uses functors and natural transformations. The Abelian category track uses zero objects, limits, and colimits. Both tracks can start before the Kan extension work is complete. Share sheaf prerequisites for the later abelian applications.
4. **Verify each phase.** Compile the theorems and representative examples with minimal imports. Check key declarations with `#print axioms`. Verified proofs must be free of `sorry`, `admit`, and additional mathematical axioms. For the first CWM pass, report the number of verified main-text items and the number of cataloged exercises.

The Lake library is `ProjectEpisteme`. The proposed layout puts track-specific bridging lemmas and examples in `ProjectEpisteme/Category/`, `ProjectEpisteme/Monoidal/`, and `ProjectEpisteme/Abelian/`. General braid group results can live in `ProjectEpisteme/Braid/` and be imported by the monoidal modules. Source IDs are independent of Lean declaration names so several chapters can reuse a result.

## Questions for the pinned mathlib audit

- Determine whether to reuse an external braid group implementation. Check whether it provides the presentations, juxtaposition, and permutation maps needed and whether it works in the pinned environment.
- Determine how much new proof is needed for universal δ-functors and general long exact sequences. Use the results to refine phase 3D.
