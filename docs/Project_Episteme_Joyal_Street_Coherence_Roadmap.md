# Project Episteme — Joyal–Street Coherence Formalization Roadmap

**Status:** Proposed research and implementation roadmap\
**Date:** 15 November 2026\
**Language and foundation:** Lean 4 with mathlib4\
**Primary goal:** Formalize the Joyal–Street coherence theorem for braided monoidal categories and derive symmetric monoidal coherence as a corollary.

## 1. Mathematical objectives

The project will formalize a universal, proof-producing correspondence between structural morphisms in braided monoidal categories and elements of Artin braid groups. It will use the original Joyal–Street approach, rather than relying on the coherence argument in Mac Lane's *Categories for the Working Mathematician* (CWM).

The principal statements are:

1. **Braid category freeness (one generator).** The braid category \(\mathcal B\), with objects \(n\in\mathbb N\) and endomorphism groups \(\mathcal B(n,n)=B_n\), is the free braided strict monoidal category on one object. In a suitably formulated category of strong braided monoidal functors,
   \[
   \operatorname{BrMonFun}(\mathcal B,\mathcal C)\simeq\mathcal C.
   \]
   This is the content of Joyal–Street (1986), §4, Theorem 4, and the strict case of Joyal–Street (1991), Theorem 3.1.

2. **Colored braid category freeness (arbitrary generators).** For a type \(A\) of generating objects, construct a colored braid category \(\mathcal B_A\) and prove
   \[
   \operatorname{BrMonFun}(\mathcal B_A,\mathcal C)
   \simeq \operatorname{Fun}(\operatorname{Discrete}(A),\mathcal C).
   \]
   The next generalization replaces the discrete generating type by a generating category \(\mathcal D\), including morphisms as strand labels. This follows the construction and Theorem 3.2 of Joyal–Street (1991).

3. **Braided coherence.** For any two *parallel formal structural morphisms* \(f,g\) generated by associators, unitors, braidings, their inverses, tensor products, and composition, prove
   \[
   f=g\text{ under every braided monoidal interpretation}
   \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
   \operatorname{braid}(f)=\operatorname{braid}(g).
   \]
   The right-hand side means equality in the appropriate braid group, with compatible source and target label words. For a *fixed* braided monoidal category, braid equality implies morphism equality, but the converse need not hold; the free category detects the converse universally.

4. **Symmetric coherence as a corollary.** Add the symmetry relation \(\beta_{Y,X}\circ\beta_{X,Y}=\mathrm{id}\), corresponding to imposing \(\sigma_i^2=1\). Prove the quotient presentation
   \[
   B_n/\langle\!\langle\sigma_i^2\rangle\!\rangle\cong S_n
   \]
   and deduce that parallel formal structural morphisms are universally equal in symmetric monoidal categories if and only if their underlying permutations agree. This is a derived result, **not a separate coherence proof**.

The first three objectives provide an algebraic coherence theorem. Geometric completeness with respect to three-dimensional ambient isotopy is an additional, explicitly separate goal.

## 2. Proof strategy from the original papers

### 2.1 Artin's group presentation

For each \(n\), define the group generated by \(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_{n-1}\), subject to

\[
\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i=\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1},
\qquad
\sigma_i\sigma_j=\sigma_j\sigma_i\quad(|i-j|\ge2).
\]

Prove the universal property of this presentation, the canonical permutation homomorphism \(B_n\to S_n\), and **juxtaposition** of braids \(B_m\times B_n\to B_{m+n}\). The far-commutativity relations justify the functorial interchange law for juxtaposition. Verify that \(B_0\) and \(B_1\) are trivial and \(B_2\cong\mathbb Z\).

### 2.2 From braidings to braid generators

For a braided monoidal category \(\mathcal C\), interpret the generator \(\sigma_i\) by applying \(\beta\) to the two adjacent tensor factors and identities to the others. Work with a canonical bracketing, inserting associators and unitors where necessary.

The proof obligations are precisely those in Joyal–Street (1986), §4:

- The **distant-commutativity relation** follows from bifunctoriality of the tensor product.
- The **Yang–Baxter relation** follows from braiding naturality and the two hexagon identities.
- Inverses are interpreted by inverse braidings.
- Block crossings agree with the given braiding by the hexagon identities, proved inductively.

For *different* labels \(X_i\), a braid can change the order of the tensor factors. It must therefore be interpreted as an arrow between the corresponding tensor words, not merely as an element of \(\operatorname{Aut}(X^{\otimes n})\). This is a reason to introduce colored braids early.

### 2.3 Free colored braids and the universal property

Start with finite lists of colors as objects. A morphism \(\vec a\to\vec b\) consists of a braid on \(n\) strands, together with the condition that its underlying permutation transports \(\vec a\) to \(\vec b\). Composition is braid multiplication; tensor product is concatenation of words and juxtaposition of braids. The block-crossing braid provides the braiding.

Construct the interpretation functor into an arbitrary braided monoidal category from an assignment of generating objects. Establish its functor laws, strong monoidality, and preservation of braiding. Then prove the universal property, including its action on monoidal natural transformations (full faithfulness of evaluation/restriction, not just existence on objects).

For a generating *category* \(\mathcal D\), a labeled braid additionally carries a morphism of \(\mathcal D\) along each strand, as in Joyal–Street (1991), Theorem 3.2. Naturalities become part of the categorical composition laws. This upgrade is essential for full string-diagram reasoning with non-structural generating morphisms.

### 2.4 Deriving coherence

Interpret formal parenthesized tensor expressions and structural arrows in the free colored braid category. Associators and unitors become identity braids; braidings become block crossings.

Prove both directions separately:

- **Soundness:** Equal underlying braids have equal interpretations in every braided monoidal category, by the free category's universal property.
- **Completeness:** If the underlying braids differ, evaluate the formal arrows in the free colored braid category; the two morphisms remain distinct.

This avoids confusing equality in a particular category with equality forced by the braided monoidal axioms.

### 2.5 Symmetric quotient

Impose \(\sigma_i^2=1\), identify the resulting Coxeter type-\(A\) presentation with \(\operatorname{Equiv.Perm}(\operatorname{Fin}\,n)\), and construct the free colored permutation category as a quotient of the free colored braid category.

Prove the symmetric universal property and its coherence criterion by factoring braided interpretations through this quotient. **The quotient identification is a genuine proof obligation**: merely observing that every symmetric braiding is involutive does not by itself prove the completeness direction.

## 4. Critical risks and design decisions

1. **The one-generator theorem is not the full colored theorem.** The passage from one repeated object to arbitrarily labeled tensor factors must be proved. Joyal–Street (1991), Theorem 3.2, supplies the proper generalization. Do not silently infer it from the one-generator case.
2. **Strict versus weak monoidal structures.** The 1986 proof reduces the general case to a strict model. In Lean, prefer explicit normalization/rebracketing using existing mathlib coherence and carefully constructed strong monoidal functors. If a strictification theorem is used, formalize and verify its braided compatibility rather than assume it.
3. **Dependent indexing and permutations.** Braid endpoints, word lengths, label transport, and multiplication order should be encoded explicitly. In particular, verify the chosen convention for the homomorphism \(B_n\to S_n\) against categorical composition.
4. **External braid-group formalization.** Hannah Fechtner's 2026 Lean work contains presentations, finite braid groups and a verified word solver. Assess the precise code version, license, and mathlib compatibility before reuse. The full solver is optional until Phase 6.
5. **CWM as a non-dependency.** The reported concern about the CWM proof should be addressed by an independent, line-by-line audit if required. This roadmap does not claim to have isolated a specific erroneous line. No phase may rely on an unproved transition in CWM.
6. **Algebraic versus geometric coherence.** Identifying braids by an algebraic presentation does not yet prove that the formalized object agrees with ambient isotopy classes in three-dimensional space. Keep that comparison as a separate theorem, with its own topology prerequisites.

## 5. Verification matrix

The following tests are mathematically important, not merely examples of tactic performance:

| Test | Required result |
| --- | --- |
| \(\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i=\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1}\) | Provable in \(B_n\) |
| \(\sigma_i\sigma_j=\sigma_j\sigma_i\) for distant indices | Provable in \(B_n\) |
| \(\sigma_1\sigma_1^{-1}=1\) | Provable in \(B_2\) |
| \(\sigma_1^2=1\) in \(B_2\) | **Not provable**; establish \(B_2\cong\mathbb Z\) or another separating invariant |
| \(s_1^2=1\) in \(S_2\) | Provable in the symmetric quotient |
| Two distinct braids with the same permutation | Remain distinct in the free braided category; become equal in the appropriate symmetric quotient |
| Structurally different parenthesizations with identical braid | Provably equal after coherent rebracketing |
| Distinct color words with incompatible endpoints | No incorrectly typed interpretation or accidental equality |

For every completed phase: run Lean's kernel checker, keep the intended theorems free of `sorry` and extra axioms, and add small regression tests for orientation, types, and naturality.

## 6. Longer-term extensions

Once algebraic braided and symmetric coherence have been verified, extend toward the geometric part of Joyal–Street (1991):

1. The free braided category on a **tensor scheme**, including generating morphisms (Theorem 3.7).
2. Semantics of progressive three-dimensional string diagrams and invariance under appropriate deformations.
3. Comparison of algebraic braid classes with topological braids and isotopy classes.
4. Balanced/ribbon structures, later rigid and pivotal structures, and relevant tangle calculi.
5. A kernel-checked diagrammatic foundation for tensor categories and, eventually, modular tensor categories and topological phases.

These extensions must not be conflated with the initial coherence theorem: Joyal–Street coherence validates structural braid manipulations, but does not alone classify fusion categories or solve their \(F\)- and \(R\)-symbol constraints.
